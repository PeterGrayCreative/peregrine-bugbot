## Methodology corrections

The Astra xhigh methodology review supports these distinctions and controls:

- A universal human signature on every future item is not a research-wide
  requirement, but AI consensus is not independent human confirmation. Use a
  frozen representative probability/stratified sample plus all materially
  disputed or high-impact cases, record sampling probabilities and label-error
  uncertainty, and expand or remain inconclusive when triggers fire. Full
  human review remains valid for a small corrected corpus. These are
  methodology-derived controls, not evidence that any current item passed
  them. See [Gilardi et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10372638/)
  and [Prediction-Powered Inference](https://www.stat.ubc.ca/~john/papers/AngelopoulosScience2023.pdf).
- A competent baseline needs an exact frozen minimal prompt, identical raw
  scope/tools/model/effort/schema, comparable total allowances, and no
  Peregrine leakage. Equal caps do not prove equal resources consumed. A
  two-arm candidate-versus-baseline test is enough for product value; four
  arms are retained only for instruction/topology attribution. The two-arm
  minimum and four-arm attribution boundary are Peregrine policy choices
informed by the Astra xhigh methodology review.
- The 12-case/96-attempt design is exploratory. Repeats are not independent
  cases. Before confirmation, preregister the estimand, dependence unit,
  numerical or simulation/design-appropriate power/error plan, practical
  margins, interval method, multiplicity policy, and failure handling. The
  2-of-3 severe rule is a stop/investigation rule only; the 50-PR cohort is
  feasibility/signal collection only. The reviewer calculations illustrating
  low information in small samples are not Peregrine results and do not
  prescribe an exact confirmatory size. See [Cawley and
  Talbot](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/cawley10a/cawley10a.pdf),
  [Lakens, Sample Size Justification](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267),
  [Lakens, Equivalence Tests](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/),
  and [Miller](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1).
